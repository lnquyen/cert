Domain 1

The foundational vocabulary of service management: value, service, outcomes, costs, risks, utility and warranty.

Domain 1

Key Concepts of Service Management

Value co-creation, service, outcome, output, cost, risk, utility, warranty
1

Value co-creation

In ITIL 4, value is not simply "delivered" by a provider to a consumer — it is co-created through collaboration between the service provider and the service consumer.

2

Utility vs. warranty

Utility is what a service does — fitness for purpose. Warranty is how well it performs — fitness for use, covering availability, capacity, security and continuity. A service needs both to create value.

Domain 2

The four interdependent perspectives an organization must consider for effective service management.

Domain 2

The Four Dimensions of Service Management

Organizations & people, information & technology, partners & suppliers, value streams & processes
1

The four dimensions

Every product and service should be viewed through four dimensions: Organizations and People, Information and Technology, Partners and Suppliers, and Value Streams and Processes — neglecting any one can undermine the value created.

2

External factors (PESTLE)

All four dimensions are influenced by external factors, commonly analyzed using the PESTLE model: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental.

Domain 3

Recommendations that guide an organization in all circumstances, regardless of changes in strategy, structure or technology.

Domain 3

ITIL Guiding Principles

7 principles that apply to almost every initiative
1

Focus on value

Everything an organization does should map, directly or indirectly, to value for itself, its customers and other stakeholders.

2

Progress iteratively with feedback

Resist the temptation to do everything at once. Organize work into smaller, manageable pieces, using feedback after each iteration to adjust the next step.

3

The other five principles

Start where you are · Collaborate and promote visibility · Think and work holistically · Keep it simple and practical · Optimize and automate.

Domain 4

How an organization's components and activities work together to enable value creation.

Domain 4

Service Value System & Service Value Chain

SVS components and the 6 value chain activities
1

Service Value System (SVS) components

The SVS describes how all the components and activities of an organization work together as a system to enable value creation: Guiding principles, Governance, the Service Value Chain, Practices, and Continual improvement.

2

The six value chain activities

Plan, Improve, Engage, Design & transition, Obtain/build, Deliver & support — these combine, using different practices, to convert demand into value.

Domain 5

Sets of organizational resources for performing work, grouped by general, service, and technical management.

Domain 5

ITIL Management Practices

15 practices commonly emphasized on the exam
1

Practice categories

Practices fall into three categories: General management practices (e.g. continual improvement), Service management practices (e.g. incident management, change enablement, service desk), and Technical management practices (e.g. deployment management).

2

Incident management vs. problem management

Incident management restores normal service operation as quickly as possible. Problem management reduces the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying their actual and potential causes — a common point of exam confusion.
